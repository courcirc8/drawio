# Plan — raise the 2446 drawio schematic to reference quality

Scope: **2446 only.** The 915 SVG is not a valid target (user, 2026-08-31) — do not
tune against it, do not use it as a reference image, and do not let its numbers
gate anything here.

Baseline under test: `out/matching_2446_seeded.drawio`, produced by
`engine=v3&optimize=12&seed=matching_2446` (seed scale 2.4).

---

## 0. What the current measurement actually says (and where it lied)

Two independent measurements were run: my own, and an adversarial re-derivation
briefed only with the artifacts.

**Confirmed, and not to be re-litigated:**

- **LVS is genuinely clean.** The audit reconstructed every net from the XML
  `source=`/`target=`/`exitName` attributes, independently of `lib/lvs.js`, and
  matched all 15 devices + 5 ports + 5 grounds against `matching_2446.cir`. So
  every wire that *looks* wrong in the raster is a routing artefact, not a
  miswire. No electrical defect is in scope.
- `crossings = 2`, `through_component = 0`, `align_ratio = 0.88`. On the
  project's own agreed geometry proxies the sheet is reference-competitive.

**Two things my own measurement got wrong, corrected here:**

1. **I quoted `score_raw` as if it were a verdict.** `tools/beauty.py <xml> -`
   passes `png_path='-'`, which skips `cv_metrics()` entirely, and the JS-side
   terms (`flow` weight 22 — the largest single term — plus `rails` 10,
   `pair_sym` 8, `mirror_row` 6) are not in the CLI path at all. beauty.py
   *withholds the `score` field on purpose* whenever `missing_weight != 0`;
   54 of 107 weight was unevaluated. For a matching network, `flow` and
   `pair_sym` ARE the quality. Every `raw=` number quoted so far in this work
   is a partial score.
   - One half of that criticism does NOT hold: `cv2` is present (4.11.0) on the
     `python3` that is on PATH here — the PySpectre venv 3.11. The audit hit a
     `ModuleNotFoundError` from a different interpreter in its own shell. The
     CLI invocation was the defect, not the environment.
2. **I reported "1 DRC error" as "1 defect".** `tools/check.py` returns
   **1 error + 25 warnings** (12 `edge-hug`, 12 rule-29, 1 `label-on-wire`), and
   its 52 rules model none of: sprawl, aspect, reading order, differential
   symmetry, zone colour, callouts, or active-device symbols. An error count is
   a statement about those 52 rules, never about whether an engineer signs it.

**The defect I had missed entirely, and it is the dominant one:**

- **Sprawl.** `sprawl = 4.161` → content bbox is **5.16× the ideal area**;
  penalty 25.0 out of 52.7, about half of all penalty. Content bbox is
  **1636×1228** against the reference's **1060×640**. The `Up` net is routed as
  a ~700×350 px empty rectangle through the middle and the whole lower-right
  quadrant is blank. This is a direct consequence of `seedScale = 2.4`, which I
  chose by sweeping DRC errors without ever measuring area.

## 1. Root cause behind both the wrap-around AND the sprawl

These are one bug, not two.

In the reference, `Bp` is the **top** rail (y=70): top pin of C1, top pin of L3,
left pin of C4. In the generated sheet:

| cell | y | note |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | box 160→220, `rotation=90` | Bp pin at top, y≈140 |
| L3 | 186, `rotation=90` | top pin y≈140 |
| C4 | 40→100 | top |
| `J_Bp` | **295** | the junction dot for net Bp |
| `P_Bp` | **286** | the Bp port |

The `Bp` node sits ~150 px **below** all three pins it joins. That is why C1
reports `wrap-around`: its Bp terminal points up and its Bp node is down.

It is **not** a dipole to flip — a 180° rotation is measurably worse overall
(−100 px on that edge, +113 px on the other two). It is step 3 of
`lib/preplace.js`: cells with no seed (junction dots, ports, ground stubs are
named by NET, not by refdes) are carried by a neighbour's translation instead of
being **placed on the pin of the net they represent**.

And that same bug is why low seed scales failed: at scale 1.0–1.2 the DRC error
count was 8–16, which I attributed to "the fixed-width stencils leave the router
no corridor" and worked around with a global ×2.4 dilation. A large share of
those errors were mis-placed unseeded cells. **Fix the carry and the dilation may
become unnecessary — which is exactly what kills the sprawl.**

## 2. Work items

Ordered by (evidence strength × cheapness). Each states its gate.

### A — Place unseeded cells on their net's pin, not by carried offset
*Class: placer. Cost: tool-level, one-time. File: `lib/preplace.js` step 3.*

For a junction dot / port / ground of net N, compute the target from the seeded
components that actually carry N: place it at the centroid of their N-pins
(absolute, post-rotation), nudged clear of bodies. Fall back to the current
carry only when no seeded component carries N.

**Gate:** `J_Bp` lands within one stub of the C1/L3/C4 Bp-pin row; `wrap-around`
error count 1 → 0.

### B — Re-sweep `seedScale` with area as a first-class metric
*Class: measurement. Cost: a sweep, no new code.*

Only after A. Sweep 1.0…2.4 recording, per point: check.py errors AND warnings,
content bbox, `sprawl`, crossings, and the FULL beauty score.

**Gate:** pick the smallest scale that holds `errors == 0`; target
`sprawl < 1.0` (bbox ≤ 2× ideal). Do not accept a scale on error count alone —
that is the mistake being corrected.

### C — Fix the measurement harness before trusting any of B
*Class: tooling. Cost: small. Blocks B.*

- Drive `beauty` through `lib/beauty.js scoreDocument()` (full path: renders,
  computes the JS terms, runs the cv2 terms) instead of `tools/beauty.py <xml> -`.
- Treat a non-empty `missing_terms` as **inadmissible**, not as a low score —
  `compare()` in beauty.py already encodes exactly this rule.
- Report errors AND warnings, never errors alone.

### D — Two certain emitter bugs
*Class: label/glyph emitter. Cost: tool-level, small.*

- `formatComponentValue('R','0.0')` → `"0"`, dropping the unit; reference says
  `0 Ω`. The zero branch loses the suffix.
- **Five nets are labelled twice**: `Bp`, `Bn`, `rx_Bp`, `rx_Bn`, `ANT` each
  carry their name on the port glyph *and* on the wire reaching it
  (`P_Bp` + `w3052`, …). The reference names each once. Two glyph conventions
  for one node is the "is this one net or two?" hazard both visual reviews hit.
  Suppress the wire label when a port vertex already carries that net's name.

**Gate:** `R_ant0` renders `0 Ω`; each net name appears exactly once;
LVS still true (the wire label feeds `connectivity()` net-name recovery — the
port label must be proven to serve that role before the wire label is dropped).

### E — Router: 12× `edge-hug` and 12× rule-29
*Class: router. Cost: tool-level, medium. Do LAST.*

- `edge-hug`: taps running along capacitor plate flanks read as part of the
  symbol (C1, C2, C6, C8, C9, C10, C12). Needs a plate-axis clearance in the
  router.
- rule-29: same-net collinear segments drawn twice, up to 267 px on `Up`
  (w3057/w3059) and 158 px on `Bp` (w3052/w3053). Needs a same-net segment merge.

**Gate:** both counts strictly down, `errors` still 0, LVS still true.

### F — The intent gap: an input convention
*Class: netlist genuinely cannot carry it. Cost: new convention. Separate work item.*

`matching_2446.cir` has **no comments at all** — 15 component lines and `.end`.
So none of this can be derived, and no placer improvement will produce it. The
full inventory, already extracted mechanically from the reference SVG:

- three zone colours: PA `#cf3b2e` (C1/L3/C2/C4), LNA `#1f6feb`
  (C6/L5/L7/C8/C9), shared `#7a4fbf` (C10/L11/C12/C13/R_ant0/L14);
- value suffixes: `DC BLOCK` (C13), `ESD` (L14), `BRIDGE` (R_ant0);
- 8 free annotations with position and size: `PA`, `LNA`, `gates x4`,
  `bondwire` ×2, `50 Ω`, `ANT`, `Pi1 shared`, and the note
  `PA internally shunted, 1.5 ohm each leg`;
- two amplifier block symbols (PA, LNA) that no stencil currently emits.

Proposed channel: extend `seeds/matching_2446.json`, which is **already** the
"reference intent" sidecar. It touches neither `matching_2446.cir` (frozen, BOM
ground truth) nor the builders.

**Explicitly deferred**: F is a different work item from A–E. A–E are "the
placer/router/emitter is wrong"; F is "the input does not carry the information".
Do not report A–E as closing the retirement bar — the bar is
*faster to fix than to redraw by hand*, and with F open, a hand redraw stays
competitive because `draw_matching_2446.py` already exists and produces the
reference.

## 5. Round result — 2026-08-31

### A–E: gate met, and the loop converged

| | start of round | end of round |
|---|---|---|
| `check.py` | 1 err / 25 warn | **0 err / 12 warn** |
| bbox | 1636×1228 | 947×713 (ref 1060×640) |
| `sprawl` | 4.161 | **0.735** |
| crossings | 2 | 1 |
| admissible `score` | 33.4 | 51.9 |
| LVS | true | true |

`seedScale` = **1.3** (was 2.4), swept under the CORRECTED checker; the only point
reaching 0 errors while holding `sprawl < 1.0`.

**Two regressions introduced during the round, both mine, both fixed:**

- The step-2b orientation pass used `rotation = (cur + 180) % 360`. Electrically
  free, but mxGraph rotates the LABEL with it (mxText shares the cell state's
  rotation) — `C6 / 300 fF` rendered upside down. Replaced with `flipH=1`, which
  is applied in mxShape's paint only and which mxGraph mirrors the connection
  constraint alongside, so `exitX`/`entryX` keep naming the same pin.
  **Trap:** `flip` MUST be initialised from what place3 already emitted
  (`place3.js:287` sets `flipH=1` itself). Starting from `false` DELETED place3's
  own flips — measured 0 → 4 DRC errors, crossings 1 → 9.
- `labelBackgroundColor=#ffffff`, added the day before to stop a wire striking
  its own net label, is painted OPAQUE by mxGraph: it erased ~12 px of conductor
  under every net name, and the next visual review read it as an OPEN circuit.
  A false open is a false electrical claim; struck text is only ugly. Reverted;
  `route.js::offsetEdgeLabels()` displaces the label perpendicular to its segment
  AFTER routing instead — the only point where that direction is measurable
  rather than assumed.

**Item E is deliberately NOT finished, and should not be.** The 6 remaining
rule-29 warnings are conductors *exactly superimposed* — `mergeSameNet` excludes
them by construction (`if (d < 1 …) continue`; it aligns near-parallel runs, it
does not fuse collinear ones). Superimposed, they print as ONE wire, and an
open-brief visual review reported nothing at any of those six coordinates.
Grinding them down optimises a counter the reader cannot see, which is the exact
error §0 of this plan exists to correct.

### F: shipped as an annotation layer

An open-brief visual review, told nothing about this plan, independently ranked
the F items as the sheet's top four defects. So F was built:
`lib/annotate.js`, driven by an `annotations` key in `seeds/matching_2446.json`.

Delivered: 15 zone colours (PA `#cf3b2e` / LNA `#1f6feb` / shared `#7a4fbf`),
3 value suffixes (`DC BLOCK`, `ESD`, `BRIDGE`), 9 free-text callouts, 2 decorative
PA/LNA blocks.

**Why it is electrically inert, which is the load-bearing part:**
`components.js::classify()` keys off `style.shape` / `style.apiShape`; with
neither present it returns `{role:'other'}`, and `connectivity()` (netlist.js:85)
`continue`s on any role that is not junction/ground/power/port/component. No
annotation cell carries either key, so it is *structurally* unreachable from
LVS/BOM rather than excluded by convention. Annotations are also added AFTER
`routePage()`, so they cannot perturb a route; `annotate.js` nudges each text
along an expanding ring to stay clear of the already-frozen wire polylines.

Measured with annotations: LVS true, **0 err / 12 warn (byte-identical rule
breakdown)**, crossings 1, `missing_terms` empty, 38/38 tests.

**Do not quote the sprawl drop 0.735 → 0.16 as a quality gain.** `sprawl`
normalises bbox area against an ideal derived from the component COUNT, and the
annotations raised `n_components` 25 → 27; the metric got easier, the drawing did
not get tighter (bbox in fact grew 947×713 → 1269×758). The admissible score went
DOWN, 51.9 → 49.6, on `align_ratio` 0.80 → 0.741 and `too_close` 2 → 3. That is
the honest number: the intent layer costs ~2.3 points of geometric beauty and is
worth it.
